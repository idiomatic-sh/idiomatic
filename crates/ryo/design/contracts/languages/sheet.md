## 1. Owns
Each crate owns the facts of one language: how its files are recognised, parsed by its parser library, walked into the
tree and filled into the catalog's relations, and how its manifests are read. One thing per crate (one language). What
the draft takes out is not a language's: its second per-file contract (`ParseContract`, which only wraps the walk), the
SDK as its dependency, and 30 files that hold only a comment.

## 5. The drafted public interface
The listing is `languages/api.txt` beside this sheet. **Product surface: 15 items** (main 15, tip 19), **test support:
113 items** behind the `fixtures` feature (main 111, tip 115; 60 of them the per-crate case harness `cases`, unchanged;
python's walk and grown fixtures 20). Counted by `pub` lines, enum variants not counted. Nothing in it is agreed.
By tag: product 9 `[changed]` (`linked`), 1 `[changed]` (`linked_with_settings`), 5 `[main]` (python's settings).

**The claim it starts from.** Appendix A 3.21: `pub fn linked() -> LanguageConnection` (today `-> ryo::languages::Language`);
`walk()` on python, typescript and rust "stays or goes on the owner's word". Checked: true, and it is not all that changes
in the nine, because the outbound/language draft removes `ParseUseCase` and moves the author surface out of the SDK.

**5a. What each language exports** (all nine the same, decision 1):
- `pub fn linked() -> ryo_language::LanguageConnection`, made by `ryo_language::link!(name, walk: .., project: ..)`
  (outbound/language api.txt, section 2). The `parse:` argument goes; `link!` wraps the walk once in `NumberedWalk`.
- python keeps `linked_with_settings(PythonParserSettings)` and `settings::{PythonTargetVersion, PythonParserSettings}`:
  the Ruff benchmark sets the target version through them (section 8). Its other answers are unchanged.
- `walk()`, `walk_with_settings()` leave the product surface (decision 2).

**5b. What each language implements** (the outbound/language listing, section 1): `ryo_language::WalkUseCase` with
`Fillers` (through `ryo_language::fills!`) on its `<Language>Walk<P>`, and `ryo_language::ProjectUseCase` on its
`ProjectContract`. Its `ParseContract` (`inbound/contracts/parse/contract.rs`, 94 to 142 lines) is removed: its methods
are the walk's (`recognizes(path, explicit)`, `skips`, `capabilities`, `configuration_identity`, `unknown_reasons`,
`KIND_COUNT`, `vocabulary()`, `uses_project`). Python's `uses_project` moves to its walk as `true` (perf row 4 depends on
it). Python's fills now under `inbound/contracts/parse/readers/` move to `inbound/contracts/walk/` unchanged.

**5c. What changes inside, in all nine at once** ("a single contract, never two"): `ryo::languages::X` -> `ryo_language::X`
(1,456 sites), `ryo::{fills, reads, link}!` -> `ryo_language::{fills, reads, link}!`; the walk writes the store's tree
through `ryo_table::TreeWrite` where it writes `NodeTableBuilder` today (each language's 1 to 2 crate-internal writer traits
keep their shape, their sink changes); a filler's rows go to `&mut impl Rows<Row>` instead of `&mut dyn Rows<Row>` (the
generated code, no hand edits); `FileWords` becomes the run's `SymbolLayer` if the symbols sheet's decision 3 is taken.
The 47 crate-internal traits stay crate-internal (the ledger's fate: carried).

**5d. Test support** (behind `fixtures`, same in all nine, decision 2): `cases` as today; `fixtures::expected() ->
ryo_language::contract::Fixtures` (the kit moves from the SDK's `languages::contract` to the language crate, outbound/language
5c); `fixtures::walk() -> impl ryo_language::WalkUseCase` in every language, which replaces the three public `walk()` and the
`fixtures::contracts::parse() -> impl languages::Parse` of go, rust and python. Python's walk fixtures keep their names; the
four that take `&NodeTable` take the store's tree.

**5e. What stops being public:** `walk`, `walk_with_settings` (python), `walk` (typescript, rust) from the crate root;
`fixtures::contracts::parse` (go, rust, python). Nothing else of main's surface goes.

**How it is built.** An edge calls `ryo_<language>::linked()` and collects the nine into the list the lint crate takes
(`LintParts.languages: Vec<ryo_language::LanguageConnection>`, inbound/lint api.txt). No other constructor.

## 10. Decisions for the owner
**1. All nine move in one landing: `linked() -> ryo_language::LanguageConnection`, two contracts implemented, `ParseContract` gone.**
Example: go builds its walk inside `ParseContract` (`contract.rs:20`) and again for `link!` (`lib.rs:40`); python answers
`uses_project` `true` in one copy and `false` in the other. Why: "a single contract, never two"; the copies drift. What we
get: each language implements `Walk` and `Project` and links in one call; 1,456 mechanical path changes, no change to what
any language fills. **My pick:** yes, all nine in the same landing as outbound/language.

**2. `walk()` leaves the product surface; every language gets `fixtures::walk()`.**
Example: `ryo_python::walk()` has one caller, a bench no target builds; typescript's and rust's have only doctests; six
languages have none. Why: one shape of a linked language; a second constructor is a second way in. What we get: the product
surface is `linked()` (and python's settings) in all nine; the growth harness and the per-relation fill bench can run for
all nine through the same test-support call, with the walk's type fixed at compile time. **My pick:** yes.

**3. Restore what held the contract: the allocation test and the provider bench in all nine.**
Example: the eight commits that removed the old readers also removed `tests/allocation.rs` and `benchmarks/provider`, while
go's bench was re-pointed at the walk in the same series; python's `fillers` bench is in the tree but built by nothing.
Why: "what we dont want to lose are the perf gains and the benchmarks we wrote"; the language spec still requires the
allocation test. What we get: per-language read cost and allocation held again on the walk. **My pick:** yes, restated on
the walk, as go's bench is; plus the python `fillers`/`manifest_reading` and typescript `embedded`/`manifest_reading`
workloads under the restored `[[bench]]`.

**4. One dependency: `ryo_language` re-exports the words a language writes.**
Example: a filler names `languages::Annotates`, `CatalogRelation`, `Count`, `FileWords`, `NodeTableBuilder` today, all
through the SDK. Options: (a) the language crates depend on `language` only and it re-exports the catalog, table, symbols,
operators and store names its signatures use; (b) each language depends on `language`, `relations`, `table`, `symbols`,
`operators` and `store` directly. Why: (a) is the owner's words and Appendix A; (b) makes five more edges to hold. **My
pick:** (a); the outbound/language listing gains a re-export section (it is that sheet's change, not this one's).

**5. The 30 comment-only files go with the move.**
Example: `domain/binding_resolution/service.rs` (8 lines, a doc comment), named by perf rows 31 and 44 as where a gain lives.
Why: they hold no code and mislead (the rule against hollow artifacts); they are not contracts. What we get: the moves of
the work order do not carry empty files. **My pick:** yes, listed `left-behind` in `moves.tsv` with this reason.
